Mount Adi Kailash
(Chhota Kailash · 5,945m)

The primeval meditation seat of Lord Shiva located in the remote Vyas Valley of Uttarakhand. Revered as one of the sacred Panch Kailash peaks and the second most holy mountain in Sanatana Dharma.

🏔️ Elevation: 5,945 m (19,505 ft) 📍 Vyas Valley, Pithoragarh, Uttarakhand 🚙 Reachable by 4x4 (Road up to Jolingkong) 🛡️ Inner Line Permit Mandatory

The Divine Abode & Mythological Heritage

Mount Adi Kailash, often affectionately revered as Chhota Kailash or Baba Kailash, is an extraordinary pyramidal snow peak nestled in the high-altitude frontier of Pithoragarh district in Kumaon, Uttarakhand. In Hindu scripture, it is considered the oldest and most ancient abode where Mahadeva meditated before his celestial marriage to Devi Parvati.

Unlike Mount Kailash across the border in Tibet, Adi Kailash is situated completely within the sovereign territory of India, allowing devotees to complete their sacred pilgrimage without requiring Chinese visas or passport border stamps.

Puranic Reference in Manaskhand (Skanda Purana):

"He who takes darshan of Adi Kailash and offers prayers at Parvati Sarovar achieves the eternal grace of Shiva and Parvati, washing away sins of seven lifetimes. Even the great demon king Ravana performed intense tapasya on these very slopes to seek the blessings of Mahadev."

During their period of exile (Agyatvas), the five Pandava brothers alongside Draupadi crossed through Vyas Valley and meditated near the foot of Adi Kailash, leaving their divine marks in the adjacent peaks known as Pandav Parvat and Brahma Parvat.

Sacred Spots Around Mount Adi Kailash

When you reach the high alpine plateau of Jolingkong, multiple divine shrines and high-altitude glacial lakes surround the base of the sacred mountain:

1. Parvati Sarovar

Altitude: 4,500 m / 14,760 ft

A high-altitude turquoise glacial lake that offers a flawless mirror reflection of Mount Adi Kailash during morning hours. On its shore stands an ancient stone temple dedicated to Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati where pilgrims perform special pujas and parikrama.

2. Gauri Kund

Altitude: 4,680 m / 15,350 ft

Located higher up from Parvati Sarovar at the very foot of the Adi Kailash moraine. Legend says Goddess Parvati bathed in these holy emerald glacial waters. Yatris collect this holy Sarovar Jal to take back to their homes.

3. Jolingkong Base Camp

Altitude: 4,550 m / 14,950 ft

The ultimate staging basecamp for the yatra. Features basic ITBP post, KMVN alpine huts, and traditional tented camps surrounded by massive glaciated Himalayan walls.

4. Brahma & Pandav Parvat

Altitude: 5,600+ m

Flanking the valley are the sacred sentinels of Brahma Parvat and the rugged ridgelines of the Pandava brothers, creating a natural Himalayan fortress of divine silence.

How to Reach Mount Adi Kailash

Until recently, reaching Adi Kailash required an arduous 80 km trek over several days. Today, thanks to the remarkable road engineering by the Border Roads Organisation (BRO), 4x4 vehicles can drive directly up to Jolingkong Basecamp, making this holy pilgrimage accessible to senior citizens, families, and devotion-driven yatris alike.

Standard Travel Route:

Kathgodam / Haldwani → Almora → Pithoragarh → Dharchula (Basecamp & ILP Clearance) → Malpa → Chialekh Pass → Gunji / Nabi Village (Acclimatization Stay) → Jolingkong (Adi Kailash Base).

For pilgrims seeking the ultimate comfort or those constrained by time, VIP Helicopter Packages operate from Pithoragarh (Naini Saini Airport) directly to the high-altitude heli-pads at Gunji/Jolingkong.

Best Season to Visit Mount Adi Kailash

The pilgrimage season is officially open from May to October:

  • May – June (Spring & Summer): Fresh snow deposits on the peaks, pleasant daytime temperatures (10°C to 18°C), and blooming alpine wildflowers along the trails.
  • July – August (Monsoon): Rains in the lower valleys between Kathgodam and Dharchula; travel requires experienced mountain drivers and buffer days.
  • September – October (Autumn Post-Monsoon): The absolute best time for crystal-clear azure skies, razor-sharp views of Adi Kailash, and flawless mirror reflections in Parvati Sarovar. Daytime temperatures (5°C to 14°C), night freezing.
